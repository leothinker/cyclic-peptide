------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE FINDING
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  In every one of the events the excluded ink is a wide, short
  mark sitting immediately LEFT of the detector box, at the start of
  a wrapped printed line. It is the leading '=' of expressions such
  as C(=O), and the detector box begins just after it.

  side   left = 215   right = 0
  inked rows per event  : {'8': 215}
  inked cols per event  : [(24, 1), (28, 5), (29, 13), (30, 18), (31, 40), (32, 65), (33, 42), (34, 22), (35, 8), (36, 1)]

  The height is 8 inked rows in every single event, i.e. two strokes
  of four rows: that is the shape of '='. The width spread 24-36 px
  is print and scan variation on the same glyph.

  This is a systematic detector-stage defect, not a per-cell
  accident: it recurs wherever the printed line wraps next to an
  '='.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY NOT COUNTED AS A DEFECT
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  148 clean cells have a left overhang of 3-10 px.
  That ink is the left edge of an ordinary glyph such as 'C'
  sitting a few px outside its box. It is narrow and tall, the
  glyph is still complete, and the recogniser still reads it.
  It is NOT character loss and is not counted as such.

  contact_top is 'yes' on 335/335 cells.
  That is the cell's own top border rule, one printed pixel of
  horizontal rule at the very top of every crop. It is grid
  contamination, not a clipped glyph, and carries no signal.

  contact_left is 'yes' on 1 cell (cmpd 2173).
  Inspected: the corner pixel of that cell's top border rule, not
  a cut glyph. See probe_2173_left_edge.png.

------------------------------------------------------------------------
DISTINCTION FROM AN OCR ERROR
------------------------------------------------------------------------
  This audit measures geometry only. It never re-ran the recogniser
  and never scored CER, so it makes no claim about how many
  characters were misread overall.
  What it establishes is narrower and firmer: for the affected
  cells a character is physically absent from the pixels handed to
  the recogniser. No recogniser, and no amount of candidate
  rescoring, can recover a glyph that was never in its input.
