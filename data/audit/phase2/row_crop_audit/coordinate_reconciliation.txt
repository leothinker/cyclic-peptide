========================================================================
   detector box x           : [41, 1667]
   OLD full row ink         : [5, 1666]
   ROW full width, no rules : [7, 1666]
   NEW boxed window ink     : [11, 1666]
   NEW window left edge     : 11
   NEW measurement censored : yes

   ink left of box, ROW defn: 34 px  <-- exact

   RESOLUTION
   - The OLD left number counts the cell's own border rule, so it
     overstates the clipped width. It is not a glyph measurement.
   - The NEW left number is 30 px on line 2 against 0-5 px on every
     other line, which is why line 2 stands out at all.
   - LIMITATION THAT MATTERS: on line 2 the NEW number sits
     exactly on its own search window edge, so it is a CENSORED
     lower bound and the true overhang may be larger. The
     previous report stated 30 px as if it were exact.
   - The old experiment never examined the left edge at all. Its
     deficit column is ink_x2 minus box_x2, a RIGHT edge statistic,
     and its conclusion was scoped to that side. So there is no
     genuine contradiction between the two reports to resolve.

   NOT CLAIMED HERE
   This script measures GEOMETRY ONLY. That a printed '=' is lost
   on line 2 is supported elsewhere, by the visual zoom in
   recognizer_candidates/ and by the CTC likelihood, but overhang
   alone does not establish which character, if any, was cut.

6. GLYPH OR GRID RULE?  (decides whether overhang means a lost char)
========================================================================

An overhang of a few pixels is ordinary detector slack. An overhang
equal to a whole glyph is a lost character. The two must not be
counted together, so the ink left of the box is characterised here.

line  overhang  ink_cols  ink_rows                      shape                reading
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   0         2         2        30        30 rows over 2 cols glyph edge, detector slack
   1         4         4        36        36 rows over 4 cols glyph edge, detector slack
   2        34        34         8     8 strokes over 34 cols   whole glyph excluded
   3         3         3        15        15 rows over 3 cols glyph edge, detector slack
   4         1         1        26        26 rows over 1 cols glyph edge, detector slack
   5         6         6        44        44 rows over 6 cols glyph edge, detector slack
   6         5         5        46        46 rows over 5 cols glyph edge, detector slack

Reading of line 2 specifically
  The ink at x=6..42 is not a table rule. A rule would be one long
  stroke; this is TWO strokes four pixels thick, eight pixels
  apart, spanning the same 37 columns - the shape of '='. It sits
  vertically inside line 2's own box, so it belongs to line 2, and
  it stops at x=42, two columns before the following 'O' begins at
  x=45. That is exactly the leading '=' of the printed 'C(=O)'.

  The detector box starts at x=41, so the glyph is excluded almost
  entirely: the recognised line 2 text begins 'O)N(C)...'.

  By contrast the 1-6 px overhang on the other six lines is the left
  edge of a 'C' sitting marginally outside its box, with the glyph
  still fully present. See probe_line0_leftmost_ink.png for the
  contrast. Those rows are NOT flagged as character loss.

========================================================================
7. TWO DIFFERENT THINGS CALLED 'LEFT PADDING'
========================================================================

  1036_pad_compare_all.png adds WHITE margin to the left of the
  unchanged box interior. The '=' stays absent, because it was
  never inside the box. This shows what the recogniser would get
  if the box were merely given more surrounding whitespace.

  1036_left_extension.csv, from the previous round, reports that
  pad_left of 16/30/36 px made line 2 read '-O)N(C)CC(=O)' instead
  of 'O)N(C)CC(=O)'. That is a DIFFERENT operation: it re-cut the
  row from the parent image starting further left, so the '='
  pixels genuinely entered the crop and were then misread as '-'.

  The two are not in conflict and must not be quoted against each
  other. White padding cannot restore a glyph; re-cropping can put
  the glyph back but the recogniser still calls it '-'.
